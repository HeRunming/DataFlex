\begin{abstract}
Targeted instruction selection uses query-derived signals to identify training
examples useful for a target task. An improved proxy value may still fail to
predict downstream utility. We introduce Directional Second-Moment Coreset
(\method{}), a greedy selector with a directly measurable target-gradient
discrepancy. On a controlled MMLU stress test, \method{} outperforms
round-robin baselines with Llama-2-7B. The advantage reverses with
Llama-3.2-3B. BBH provides the central counterexample. Across three shared
query/selection draws in each of two model stacks, \method{} attains the lowest
observed target discrepancy among the compared subsets and underperforms
Random in every draw ($-2.94$ points on Llama-2-7B; $-0.89$ on
Llama-3.2-3B). The \method{}--Random discrepancy ordering persists under
post-hoc rescoring with evaluation-matched gradients and with a 256-example
proxy set disjoint from every selection query. A signed first-moment selector
exhibits the same qualitative reversal. The operational teacher-forced query
loss can also improve with no corresponding chain-of-thought gain. In the
tested final-answer-gradient targeting pipelines, lower operational first- or
second-moment discrepancies therefore fail to rank downstream utility
reliably.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Instruction tuning \citep{wei2022finetuned} draws from heterogeneous pools,
whereas deployment may require a narrow capability. Targeted selection uses a
small query set to score candidate data through distribution alignment
\citep{liu2024tsds}, gradient similarity or influence
\citep{xia2024less,wang2025nice}, target subspaces \citep{min2026gist}, or
task-metric signals \citep{wang2025nice,wu2025rose}. We ask a narrower question
about attained proxy values. Recent work studies which representations and
selectors are predictive on average \citep{nayak2026critical}. Our focus is
whether a measured discrepancy ordering reliably ranks the utility of the
compared subsets.
For a fixed candidate pool, budget, training procedure $A$, and family of
compared subsets, call the proxy \emph{order-preserving} when
\begin{equation}
    D(S_1,Q)<D(S_2,Q)
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    U(A(S_1))\ge U(A(S_2)).
    \label{eq:ordering}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:ordering} serves as a diagnostic definition for this paper.
Useful selectors may satisfy only weaker average predictive relationships. Our
experiments examine the stronger interpretation often attached to an attained
proxy improvement: that it ranks the compared subsets correctly.

A downstream score alone cannot distinguish failure to attain a proxy from
failure of the proxy itself. We address this ambiguity with a measurable
instrument. For unit-normalized projected gradients $u$, represent a set by
\begin{equation}
    M_P = \mathbb{E}_{u\sim P}[uu^\top].
\end{equation}
Under the quadratic kernel $k_2(u,v)=(u^\top v)^2$, empirical maximum mean
discrepancy (MMD) is exactly the squared Frobenius distance between directional
second moments \citep{gretton2012mmd,muandet2017kme}. Directional Second-Moment
Coreset (\method{}) greedily reduces this discrepancy using its exact one-step
marginal, letting us verify target-geometry movement independently of utility.

The method-level results vary across stacks. On Llama-2-7B MMLU at the 5\%
budget, \method{} outperforms First-RR and Second-RR by 1.55 and 0.88 points,
with wins in all ten target-direction cells. An internally pre-specified
Llama-3.2-3B check reverses these differences ($-0.18$ and $-0.31$ points
over five paired blocks). \method{} and Random remain nearly tied on both
stacks. These results motivate using DSMC as a diagnostic instrument. Its
performance as a general-purpose selector remains unsettled.

The BBH geometry--utility reversal has the same direction in both model
stacks. Across three query/selection draws, \method{} attains the lowest
operational second-moment discrepancy and loses to Random in every draw.
The mean \method{}--Random gap is $-2.94$ percentage points on Llama-2-7B
and $-0.89$ on Llama-3.2-3B. Every BBH SFT mean lies below its no-SFT
reference. This experiment compares equally trained subsets within an observed
negative-transfer regime. The discrepancy ordering also persists on a
256-example proxy set disjoint from every selection query.

We also separate targeting loss, selection discrepancy, and downstream exact
match. On the 64 query items that define the signal, post-SFT teacher-forced
loss decreases while a chain-of-thought generation diagnostic fails to
improve. The same-item result weakens a purely query-to-test explanation.
The behavior of both this diagnostic and bare-serialization loss varies by
model stack, limiting the conclusion to the tested loss and serialization.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item We introduce \method{}, an exact-marginal greedy coreset construction
    with a directly measurable directional second-moment target discrepancy.
    \item MMLU yields opposite \method{}--round-robin rankings across the two
    model stacks: an advantage on Llama-2-7B and a reversal in the internally
    pre-specified Llama-3.2-3B check.
    \item On BBH, \method{} attains the lowest observed $\dsecond$ among the
    compared subsets in every draw yet underperforms Random in every draw on
    both model stacks. When the frozen subsets are rescored with
    evaluation-matched or query-disjoint proxy gradients, \method{} remains
    closer than Random in every draw. Same-item, parser, and task diagnostics
    constrain several explanations, while the causal mechanism remains open.
\end{itemize}

\section{Target Matching with Directional Second Moments}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Targeted instruction selection}

Let $\mathcal{C}=\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ be a candidate instruction pool and
$\mathcal{Q}=\{q_j\}_{j=1}^{M}$ a small target query set. Given a budget $K$,
the goal is to select $\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{C}$, $|\mathcal{S}|=K$, such
that supervised fine-tuning on $\mathcal{S}$ improves held-out target
performance.

Following gradient-based targeted selection
\citep{xia2024less,nayak2026critical,mirzasoleiman2020craig,killamsetty2021gradmatch},
we compute per-example gradients at a
shared warm-up checkpoint and project them to a reusable low-dimensional
datastore. Following the optimizer-aware LESS protocol, candidates and queries
use role-specific transforms before entering a common projected directional
space:
\begin{equation}
    u_{\mathcal C}(x)
    =
    \frac{\Pi A_{\mathrm{Adam}}g(x)}
         {\|\Pi A_{\mathrm{Adam}}g(x)\|_2},
    \qquad
    u_{\mathcal Q}(q)
    =
    \frac{\Pi g(q)}
         {\|\Pi g(q)\|_2}.
    \label{eq:role_maps}
\end{equation}
Here $\Pi$ is a shared Rademacher projection and $A_{\mathrm{Adam}}$ denotes
the candidate-side optimizer-aware transform. The MMD identity below is exact
for these unit vectors in the common projected space. Candidate and query
features use different transforms, so the construction describes an
operational push-forward geometry. Symmetric raw-gradient matching remains
outside the experiment.

\subsection{Why a directional second moment?}

First-order mean-gradient matching represents a target by
$\mathbb{E}[u]$. This signed mean can cancel when useful examples occupy
opposite directions, even if they share the same underlying orientation.
We instead use the non-centered directional second moment
\begin{equation}
    M_P = \mathbb{E}_{u\sim P}[uu^\top].
    \label{eq:moment}
\end{equation}
Because $uu^\top=(-u)(-u)^\top$, this representation captures orientation
and discards sign. We call it a \emph{directional second moment}: the vectors
are normalized, and the mean is not subtracted as it would be for covariance.

Consider the positive semidefinite kernel
\begin{equation}
    k_2(u,v) = (u^\top v)^2.
\end{equation}
Since
$\langle uu^\top,vv^\top\rangle_F=(u^\top v)^2$, the biased empirical MMD
between a selected set $\mathcal{S}$ and query set $\mathcal{Q}$ is exactly
\citep{gretton2012mmd,muandet2017kme}
\begin{align}
    \operatorname{MMD}_{k_2}^2(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{Q})
    &=
    \left\|
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}u_su_s^\top
    -
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|}\sum_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}u_qu_q^\top
    \right\|_F^2 \nonumber\\
    &\equiv \dsecond(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{Q}).
    \label{eq:mmd_identity}
\end{align}
This algebraic identity makes the empirical second moment directly measurable.
The quadratic kernel is non-characteristic, so $\dsecond$ does not identify
the full gradient distribution
\citep{sriperumbudur2011universality,muandet2017kme}.

\subsection{Exact greedy coreset construction}

For a candidate $x$, define target relevance and accumulated selected-set
similarity as
\begin{equation}
    r_{\mathcal Q}(x)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal Q|}
    \sum_{q\in\mathcal Q}k_2(u_x,u_q),
    \quad
    r_{\mathcal S}(x)=\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}k_2(u_x,u_s).
\end{equation}
At step $m=|\mathcal S|$, the exact one-step marginal for minimizing the biased
MMD selects
\begin{equation}
    x^\star =
    \arg\max_{x\notin\mathcal S}
    \left[
    r_{\mathcal Q}(x)
    -
    \frac{r_{\mathcal S}(x)+k_2(u_x,u_x)/2}{m+1}
    \right].
    \label{eq:greedy}
\end{equation}
For unit vectors $k_2(u_x,u_x)=1$. The first term rewards query alignment and
the second penalizes selected-set self-similarity. We use this deterministic
exact marginal throughout. The analysis invokes neither stochastic-greedy
approximations nor submodularity guarantees. The construction is related to
kernel herding and greedy kernel-mean approximation
\citep{chen2010herding,bach2012herding}.
Equation~\ref{eq:greedy} is the exact marginal of our finite-sample objective.

\paragraph{From local alignment to downstream utility.}
For one small SGD step on the same query loss,
$L_Q(\theta-\eta g_S)-L_Q(\theta)\approx-\eta g_Q^\top g_S$ motivates
alignment locally. Our pipeline additionally applies role-specific transforms,
greedy construction, multi-step Adam in a LoRA subspace, generation, and exact
match. Equation~\ref{eq:ordering} would require this chain to preserve subset
order. The signed first-order Taylor relation does not derive $\dsecond$ as a
utility surrogate or predict the full pipeline. The observed reversals remain
compatible with the local approximation.

\paragraph{Role in this paper.}
\method{} provides a controlled intervention on target geometry. A lower
$\dsecond$ with no utility gain isolates proxy quality from optimization
failure. We use DSMC for this diagnostic role. Its broader selector performance
falls outside the present study.

\section{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Candidate pool, features, and training}

The candidate pool is the processed pool released with LESS
\citep{xia2024less}: 270,679 examples from Flan v2
\citep{longpre2023flan} (100,000), its CoT mixture (100,000), Dolly
\citep{dolly2023dataset} (15,011), and OASST1
\citep{kopf2023oasst1} (55,668). For each model stack, we construct a
candidate-gradient datastore from a LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} warm-up
checkpoint. Gradients are projected to 8,192 dimensions with a Rademacher
projection (seed 123), unit-normalized, and cached separately for each query
draw.
The selected indices are materialized into static subsets. Every downstream
comparison restarts from the corresponding base model. The warm-up run supplies
features only. We study Llama-2-7B and Llama-3.2-3B model stacks
\citep{touvron2023llama2,grattafiori2024llama3,meta2024llama32},
implemented through
LLaMA-Factory \citep{zheng2024llamafactory}.

Downstream SFT uses LoRA rank 128, alpha 512, dropout 0.05 on
\texttt{q/k/v/o\_proj}, learning rate $2\times10^{-5}$, linear decay, 3\%
warm-up, bfloat16, effective batch size 128, and four epochs. The 5\% budget is
$K=13{,}533$ (420 optimizer steps) and the 1\% budget is $K=2{,}707$ (84
steps). Full configurations, prompt templates, hashes, and data-processing
details are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\subsection{Target families}

\paragraph{MMLU controlled stress test.}
We construct ten globally disjoint 64-example query sets:
five STEM-majority draws (51 STEM, 13 humanities) and five
humanities-majority draws with the proportions reversed. Evaluation is balanced
accuracy
\[
\operatorname{BalAcc}
=\tfrac12(\operatorname{Acc}_{\mathrm{STEM}}
          +\operatorname{Acc}_{\mathrm{Humanities}})
\]
over held-out MMLU subjects \citep{hendrycks2021mmlu}. We use this
equal-category aggregate in place of the benchmark's standard overall
accuracy. Draw index is paired with SFT seed $\{42,1,2,3,4\}$. For
comparisons shared across the two skew directions, the primary descriptive
unit is the five direction-averaged draw-index blocks.

\paragraph{BBH external validation.}
Using the BIG-Bench Hard suite \citep{suzgun2023bbh}, we partition the official
examples within each task family into a query reservoir and a disjoint
evaluation set. The reservoir contains 1,302 examples and the frozen held-out
set contains 5,209. The evaluation harness exposes 27 subtasks spanning
23 conceptual BBH task families, because two families contain three
size-specific variants. Three independent 64-example samples from this
per-subtask-stratified reservoir define the selection signals. Each covers
24--26 of the 27 subtasks. We report the size-weighted micro exact-match
aggregate over the custom held-out partition using a pinned chain-of-thought
few-shot protocol. Each draw is crossed with two SFT seeds, which are averaged
\emph{within} the draw. The statistical unit is therefore the draw ($n=3$),
not the six seed-level runs.

\subsection{Comparators and reporting policy}

The controlled gradient baselines include first-order mean-gradient TopK
(LESS-style \citep{xia2024less}), first- and second-order relevance TopK,
first- and second-order round-robin (RR) \citep{nayak2026critical}, GIST
whitening on the shared projected datastore
(GIST-SharedProj \citep{min2026gist}), and a task-metric adaptation of NICE
(NICE-MMLU-EM \citep{wang2025nice}). Their qualified names identify
shared-protocol adaptations; the experiments are not official end-to-end
reproductions.
Target-independent controls include uniform Random-$K$, length-matched Random
on MMLU, a sequence/label-length-matched Random control on BBH, and a shared
no-SFT reference.

The study is descriptive. We report draw-level means, paired differences, and
win counts. Samples of $n=3$ or $n=5$ blocks provide little resolution for
significance testing. Subtask breakdowns and individual SFT seeds serve as
diagnostics and are not counted as independent replicates.

\section{Method Gains Are Stack-Dependent on MMLU}
\label{sec:mmlu}

\subsection{Llama-2 positive control}

We first evaluate whether second-moment matching provides a useful
targeted-selection signal on MMLU. A single-seed exploratory $2\times2$
comparison on separate 80-example STEM- and humanities-majority targets
factors representation (signed first order versus directional second order)
and selection rule (relevance TopK versus MMD coreset). The second-order
representation improves both selectors in both directions. Its additional
MMD-over-TopK increment is $0.42$--$0.62$ points, within the measured training
noise. We treat this decomposition as a mechanism hypothesis. Appendix
~\ref{app:mmlu} reports the full exploratory comparison.

Across ten target draws at the 5\% budget,
\method{} beats LESS-style TopK and both RR baselines in every draw, and beats
the examined GIST and NICE adaptations in nine of ten draws
(Appendix~\ref{app:mmlu}). Relative to Second-RR, the mean gain is $0.88$
points and holds in all ten cells, supporting an observed coreset advantage at
this budget. This positive result applies to the joint representation--selector
construction in the Llama-2 MMLU setting.

The comparison with Random is less favorable. At 5\%, \method{} trails
Random-$K$ by
$0.01$ points on average and wins two of five direction-averaged blocks.
Length-matched Random yields the same qualitative result. At 1\%, the gap
widens to $-0.77$ points, with no query-direction cell favoring \method{}.
The MMD gain over Second-RR also contracts to $0.17$ points, with five of ten
cells favoring \method{}.

Most targeted methods fall below no-SFT at both budgets. At 1\%,
\method{} (40.17) and Random (40.95) both exceed no-SFT (40.03), while
\method{} is closer to the leave-one-draw-out balanced target geometry in all
ten draws. The discrepancy and utility rankings therefore reverse even when
both subsets improve over the base model. This deliberately skewed stress test
complements the same-family BBH validation. In an equal-step sensitivity,
repeating the 1\% subsets to 420 steps lowers both methods to approximately
38.3\% (Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivities}).

\section{Diagnosing the Geometry--Utility Reversal}
\label{sec:diagnostics}

\subsection{\method{} attains the lowest observed target discrepancy}

For every BBH draw, we compute the operational discrepancy used by \method{},
\begin{equation}
    \dsecond(\mathcal S,\mathcal Q_d)
    = \|M_{\mathcal S}-M_{\mathcal Q_d}\|_F^2.
\end{equation}
On Llama-2, mean $\dsecond$ is 0.0863 for \method{} and 0.1098 for Random;
on Llama-3.2 it is 0.0644 and 0.0752. \method{} has the lowest $\dsecond$
among the compared subsets in every draw of both stacks and lower downstream
utility than Random in those same draws. This paired 3/3 pattern is the primary
geometry result. Appendix~\ref{app:geometry} reports descriptive rank
correlations.

The selector achieves its intended discrepancy ordering relative to the
compared subsets. This statement concerns the attained greedy solution.
It does not establish global optimality. On MMLU, \method{} is also closer than
Random to a leave-one-draw-out balanced reference in all ten draws at both
budgets (Appendix~\ref{app:geometry}), despite the skewed selection queries.

\paragraph{Query-disjoint proxy geometry.}
We post-hoc score frozen subsets against 256 examples sampled after excluding
all selection queries, under a protocol fixed before proxy-gradient
extraction. Without reselection or SFT, \method{} remains closer than Random
under $\dsecond$, and First-RR under signed $D_1$, in all three draws on both
stacks. Both retain lower downstream utility. The same ordering therefore
holds when geometry is measured on examples excluded from selection
(Appendix~\ref{app:proxy_geometry}).

\paragraph{Sign-sensitive first-moment diagnostic.}
The quadratic kernel is invariant to $u\mapsto -u$, so we also examine the
signed first-moment discrepancy
\begin{equation}
    D_1(\mathcal S,\mathcal Q)
    =
    \left\|
    \mathbb E_{\mathcal S}[u]-\mathbb E_{\mathcal Q}[u]
    \right\|_2 .
\end{equation}
First-RR is closer than Random under $D_1$ in all three draws on both stacks,
yet its held-out BBH mean is lower than Random by 2.10 points on Llama-2 and
0.48 points on Llama-3.2. The observed alignment--utility reversal is
also present for this signed first-moment discrepancy. Full draw-level values
appear in Appendix~\ref{app:geometry}. The mean gaps
($0.471$ vs.\ $0.591$ and $0.459$ vs.\ $0.498$) are substantial. This
diagnostic covers one sign-sensitive representation and does not characterize
all such representations.

\paragraph{Evaluation-matched target serialization.}
To test whether the \method{} minimum is induced by the chat serialization
used for target extraction, we re-extract only target gradients under the bare
evaluation serialization and rescore frozen subsets. Despite low cross-context
row cosine (0.25--0.36), \method{} retains the lowest $\dsecond$ in all draws
on both stacks while losing to Random downstream. The full Llama-3.2 ranking
changes in every draw. The \method{}--Random counterexample survives this
serialization change, although the complete target-space ranking is
serialization-dependent (Appendix~\ref{app:evalctx}).

\subsection{The defining query loss improves}

Gradient-based targeting constructs query gradients from a differentiable
final-answer objective. We recompute its teacher-forced cross-entropy on the
same items and with the exact wrapped serialization used by each stack. On
Llama-2, all four target-aware methods reduce this loss by 0.68--1.40 nats,
while Random increases it by 0.33. On Llama-3.2, the target-aware methods
reduce it by 3.19--3.40 nats, compared with 1.37 for Random. The second stack
shows a magnitude separation, while Llama-2 also separates the methods by
sign. Across both stacks, the targeted methods improve the loss that defines
the query gradients more strongly.

\subsection{Same-item task utility diverges from query loss}

Comparing query loss to held-out exact match changes both metric and examples.
To hold the examples fixed, we run a chain-of-thought generation diagnostic on
the same 64 query items. The Llama-2 arm uses the pinned query-task harness.
The Llama-3.2 arm uses the same bare prompts with a custom greedy generator, a
512-token cap, and closely matched answer extraction. For Llama-2, every
target-aware method's mean query exact match falls relative to base
($-3.65$ to $-6.51$ points) despite the substantial cross-entropy reductions.
On Llama-3.2, all three targeted methods again have negative mean changes, with
\method{} falling by 6.51 points; Random changes by $+1.30$ points. One item
corresponds to 1.56 points, making each 64-item draw coarse. The custom
second-stack evaluator also makes those values approximate, although their mean
separation agrees with the held-out ranking.

The task metric also fails to improve on the examples that define the
targeting signal, which weakens a purely query-to-test generalization account.
Other forms of overfitting and objective mismatch remain possible, and this
diagnostic does not identify a mechanism.

\paragraph{Task-level breadth.}
The draw remains the primary experimental unit. A secondary 27-subtask
breakdown shows that \method{} trails Random on 23 subtasks and leads on four
for Llama-2 (macro difference $-2.89$ points). On Llama-3.2 it trails on 17
and leads on ten (macro difference $-1.10$). A descriptive bootstrap over
subtasks gives intervals of $[-4.68,-1.41]$ and $[-2.57,+0.27]$ points,
respectively. The Llama-2 gap is broad across subtasks; the smaller Llama-3.2
effect is more heterogeneous. Regrouping size variants into the 23 conceptual
BBH families yields the same qualitative pattern: 20/23 and 14/23 families
favor Random.

\paragraph{Parser-validity audit.}
A post-hoc audit of all frozen generations finds invalid-rate contrasts of
$+0.03$ points on Llama-2 and $-0.78$ on Llama-3.2. \method{} remains below
Random conditional on standard-valid outputs ($-3.13$, $-1.36$ points) and
under frozen conservative recovery ($-3.08$, $-0.84$), so parsing does not
account for the gap (Appendix~\ref{app:parser}).

\subsection{Serialization changes the loss diagnostic}

The targeting loss uses each stack's LLaMA-Factory wrapper
\citep{zheng2024llamafactory}, whereas BBH generation uses a pinned bare
evaluation context implemented through the language-model evaluation harness
\citep{biderman2024evaluation}. Recomputing final-answer
cross-entropy on that bare context yields different behavior across stacks. On
Llama-2, all methods increase bare-context CE, with smaller increases for the
targeted methods. On Llama-3.2, target-aware methods decrease it and Random
increases it. These results tie the loss-based finding to the tested
serialization and model stack. They show that the targeting loss can decrease
on its defining examples while chain-of-thought exact match fails to improve.
The evidence does not support a general claim about cross-entropy and
generation.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Instruction data selection.}
General-purpose instruction selection commonly scores data for quality,
difficulty, and diversity. DEITA jointly studies complexity, quality, and
diversity for data-efficient alignment \citep{liu2024deita}. Get More for Less
studies selection for the warm-up stage before target fine-tuning
\citep{kang2024getmore}. TSDS uses a small target set to define an
optimal-transport distribution-alignment objective
\citep{liu2024tsds}. Our focus is on testing whether attaining a better
query-derived proxy value is sufficient for utility.

\paragraph{Distribution matching and gradient coresets.}
MMD and kernel mean embeddings define set-level distribution discrepancies
\citep{gretton2012mmd,muandet2017kme}. Characteristic-kernel results specify
when these discrepancies identify distributions
\citep{sriperumbudur2011universality}. Kernel herding greedily approximates a
target kernel mean \citep{chen2010herding,bach2012herding}, while CRAIG and
GRAD-MATCH use gradient geometry for data-efficient training
\citep{mirzasoleiman2020craig,killamsetty2021gradmatch}. We use the established
quadratic-kernel identity and greedy moment matching as a measurable
intervention for testing the downstream value of stronger target-gradient
alignment.

\paragraph{Targeted and influence-based selection.}
LESS builds reusable low-dimensional gradient datastores and uses
optimizer-aware gradient similarity \citep{xia2024less}. NICE links influence
estimation to non-differentiable task metrics through policy gradients
\citep{wang2025nice}. GIST scores candidates in a target-gradient subspace
\citep{min2026gist}, and GIO optimizes a scalable gradient-information
objective \citep{everaert2024gio}. A controlled study that separates
representations from selectors finds useful gradient signals but no uniformly
dominant selector \citep{nayak2026critical}. Our study conditions on the
attained geometry and asks whether it ranks subset utility.

\paragraph{Surrogate mismatch and Random.}
ROSE and NICE motivate task-metric-aware selection from loss--reward mismatch
\citep{wu2025rose,wang2025nice}, while controlled studies find that complex
selectors may not consistently beat Random
\citep{diddee2025chasing,xia2025random}. Our contribution verifies proxy
attainment and traces its failure through query-disjoint geometry and
same-item metrics.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The study covers two Llama-family stacks, one candidate pool, and two target
families.
Each stack changes model, tokenizer, and serialization together. BBH has three
draws reused across stacks, and the 3B effect is small. On Llama-3.2 MMLU, all
methods are within 0.44 points and below no-SFT. MMLU also couples draw index
to seed, so five direction-averaged blocks carry shared comparisons.

Candidates use Adam-aware features and queries use raw SGD gradients. Our
claims concern this operational push-forward geometry. Symmetric raw-gradient
matching remains untested. Evaluation-matched rescoring leaves the transform
asymmetry intact, and the finer Llama-3.2 ranking changes in every draw.

Baselines are controlled adaptations, and contamination checks are lexical.
The diagnostics leave the causal mechanism open: the proxy rescores frozen
subsets, and the parser audit tests extraction. BBH targets a wrapped
final-answer loss and evaluates bare-context CoT generation, which limits the
claim to this pipeline. Protocol files document internally pre-specified
decisions. The anonymous snapshot cannot independently establish chronology.

\section{Conclusion}

DSMC's method-level advantage varies across stacks, whereas the
geometry--utility reversal has the same direction in both. The positive-transfer
MMLU 1\% comparison and the negative-transfer BBH regime each contain subsets
whose lower operational first- or second-moment gradient discrepancy coincides
with lower downstream utility. Improvements in a query-derived objective
therefore require separate validation against the downstream metric.

\section{Method Derivations}
\label{app:derivations}

\subsection{MMD identity}

Let
$\widehat M_{\mathcal S}=|\mathcal S|^{-1}\sum_{s\in\mathcal S}u_su_s^\top$
and define $\widehat M_{\mathcal Q}$ analogously. Expanding the squared
Frobenius norm gives
\begin{align}
\|\widehat M_{\mathcal S}-\widehat M_{\mathcal Q}\|_F^2
&=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal S|^2}\sum_{s,s'}
\langle u_su_s^\top,u_{s'}u_{s'}^\top\rangle_F \nonumber\\
&\quad+
\frac{1}{|\mathcal Q|^2}\sum_{q,q'}
\langle u_qu_q^\top,u_{q'}u_{q'}^\top\rangle_F \nonumber\\
&\quad-
\frac{2}{|\mathcal S||\mathcal Q|}\sum_{s,q}
\langle u_su_s^\top,u_qu_q^\top\rangle_F \\
&=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal S|^2}\sum_{s,s'}(u_s^\top u_{s'})^2
+\frac{1}{|\mathcal Q|^2}\sum_{q,q'}(u_q^\top u_{q'})^2
-\frac{2}{|\mathcal S||\mathcal Q|}\sum_{s,q}(u_s^\top u_q)^2.
\end{align}
This is the biased empirical MMD under $k_2$.

\subsection{Greedy marginal}

Terms depending only on $\mathcal Q$ can be dropped. Comparing the objective
after adding $x$ to a set of size $m$ yields Equation~\ref{eq:greedy} after
positive rescaling. The implementation maintains $r_{\mathcal S}(x)$
incrementally and masks selected or invalid rows. Zero and non-finite feature
rows are rejected before selection.

\section{Sensitivity and Falsification Checks}
\label{app:sensitivities}

\paragraph{Matched Random controls.}
MMLU length-matched Random gives the same qualitative target-awareness result
as plain Random: DSMC shows no advantage at either budget. On Llama-2 BBH,
sequence$\times$label-length-matched Random scores 38.05, between plain Random
(39.25) and \method{} (36.31). The control also changes correlated provenance
and content, precluding a causal percentage decomposition of the format
contribution.

\paragraph{Same-item and bare-context diagnostics.}
On Llama-2, targeted methods reduce the wrapped targeting loss by
0.68--1.40 nats but their same-item CoT exact match changes by
$-3.65$ to $-6.51$ points. Under the bare evaluation context, no method
improves CE. Targeted methods degrade it less than Random. On Llama-3.2,
targeted methods reduce wrapped targeting loss by 3.19--3.40 nats and also
reduce bare CE by 0.41--0.57, while their mean same-item CoT changes remain
negative. The loss-based diagnostic is therefore specific to the tested
targeting pipeline and does not characterize cross-entropy in general.

\paragraph{Task exposure.}
Per-task query frequency has negative descriptive correlations with
targeted-vs-Random subtask deltas ($-0.210$, $-0.280$, $-0.224$ across the
three draws). Greater query exposure provides no apparent protection for a BBH
task. These descriptive correlations do not identify a causal mechanism.

\section{Baseline Implementations}
\label{app:baselines}

LESS-style TopK uses one shared checkpoint and mean signed gradient similarity;
it differs from the official multi-checkpoint LESS pipeline. GIST-SharedProj
applies the paper's target-subspace whitening construction to the shared
normalized projected caches. NICE-MMLU-EM uses eight Monte Carlo samples and
an exact-match task reward in the common feature protocol. These adaptations
isolate representation and selection effects under controlled features and
are not exact end-to-end reproductions of the official systems.
